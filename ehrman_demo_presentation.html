Reader Experience

Two ways to find relevant posts

Readers can search directly when they know what they want, or browse from broad subject areas when they want to explore.

1

Keyword Search

Best for: readers who already know what they want to find.

  • Combine up to four topics or secondary keywords.
  • Use every additional term to narrow the results.
  • Limit results to a category when useful.
  • Rank by relevance or sort by date.

Why choose it: The fastest path to a known target.

Consider: Works best when readers know useful search terms.

2

Browse Topics

Best for: new readers and anyone exploring the blog.

  • Start with a broad subject area.
  • Move through categories and focused topics.
  • See descriptions and post counts along the way.
  • Reach the same posts without entering search terms.

Why choose it: Supports discovery and provides useful context.

Consider: Adds a navigation layer; two alternative structures are being tested.

The methods complement one another: Keyword Search supports precision; Browse Topics supports discovery.

Moving Forward

Next steps

Validate the content organization first, refine the reader experience through trusted testing, and then hand off a tested solution for integration.

1

Bart's content review

Confirm that the organization accurately reflects the subjects covered on the blog.

  • Validate that no important topics are missing.
  • Confirm that topics are grouped under the correct categories.
  • Choose between the two subject-area options.
2

Inner-circle testing

Use feedback from trusted readers to perfect search and browsing before handoff.

  • Test Keyword Search and Browse Topics.
  • Use both methods to find subjects of interest and confirm that each is easy to use.
  • Identify parts of the experience that are not intuitive.
  • Suggest ways to improve usability.
3

Development-team handoff

Give the developers a tested solution that is ready to integrate into Bart's blog.

  • Provide the tested WordPress plugin and organized search data.
  • Provide import scripts and integration documentation.
  • Have the development team integrate the functionality and apply the production design.

Validate the organization, perfect the reader experience, and then integrate.
